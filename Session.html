Decoding Tumor Evolution through the Human Tumor Atlas Network: An Ecological and Evolutionary Perspective

Friday, April 17, 2026 at 3:00 PM to 4:30 PM · 1 hr. 30 min. (US/Pacific)
Room 30
Educational Session
Cancer EvolutionTumor Biology

Information

Speakers will discuss how ecological and evolutionary principles, combined with the extensive data resources of the Human Tumor Atlas Network (HTAN), are informing new strategies to predict and overcome tumor progression and therapy resistance. HTAN comprises 10 research centers and a Data Coordinating Center (DCC). The session will begin with an overview of HTAN and its publicly accessible data portal, which houses all atlas datasets generated across the network. The program will then highlight representative HTAN atlases of the skin, blood, and prostate, demonstrating how these efforts are generating new biological insights and informing emerging treatment paradigms. Investigators from these centers will describe how they integrate mutational, gene expression, and proteomic datasets to construct two- and three-dimensional spatial atlases of tumors, including melanoma, squamous cell carcinoma, multiple myeloma, and prostate cancer. These atlases are uncovering tumor-intrinsic and tumor microenvironmental (TME) factors that drive critical transitions during tumor evolution. They cover the full evolutionary spectrum from normal cells to precancer, cancer, and treatment resistant tumors.
